Map sunlight and set north

Nov 12, 2026

3 min read

Beds & containers Sun Map Full sun Part sun Shade Compass North Plant shade

Record the sunlight your garden actually receives, then set the bed’s orientation to help interpret where taller plants may shade their neighbors.

Start with what you observe

A bed’s sun map records light levels across its squares. It starts as Full sun; this is a default, not a measurement.

Watch the bed over the day, including shadows from buildings, fences, and trees. Use the sun levels as a practical summary:

  • Full sun: 6 or more hours of direct sunlight.
  • Part sun: roughly 3 to 6 hours.
  • Shade: less than 3 hours.

Sunlight can change with the season. Update the map when your observations no longer match it.

Edit the sun map

Open the bed’s sun editor, choose a light level, and mark the relevant squares. Use Undo if you change a square by mistake.

Press and hold the plant-preview button to see your plants. Release it to return to the sun map.

For containers, set the sun level for each container in Edit group. Containers use their individual light settings rather than a bed-style sun grid.

Set north at the bed

In the sun editor, tap the north marker to open Set north.

  1. Stand at the bed and hold your phone flat.
  2. Point the top of the phone toward the real-world edge represented by the top of the bed layout.
  3. Turn on Use device compass and wait for a reading.
  4. Check the marker on the bed and tap Save.

The app uses a true-north reading when available. You do not need to calculate the difference from magnetic north yourself. If the reading is unavailable or unreliable, follow the message in the sheet; nearby metal or missing location access can interfere.

If you are not at the bed

You can adjust north manually with the slider if you already know the orientation. If you are unsure, leave it unset and return when you can check. A guessed direction can make plant-to-plant shade guidance less useful.

Sun exposure and plant shade are different

The map records the light available at the bed. Plant-to-plant shade guidance considers how the plants themselves may affect their neighbors, using available information such as orientation, location, season, and mature size.

An estimate of shade at mature size does not mean a newly planted seedling is casting that shadow now. The app also cannot infer surrounding buildings or trees from the compass. Keep the map grounded in observation and use the guidance to decide where a closer look would help.
