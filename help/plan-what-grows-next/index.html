Plan what grows next

Nov 12, 2026

3 min read

Plotting Planning and Planting Succession Suggested plants Timing Occupied space Earlier date Harvest

Succession planting lets you grow one plant after another in the same space. Plan what comes next while your current plants are still growing.

A succession belongs to a space

Each current planting can have up to three planned successions.

In a bed

The succession uses the current planting’s squares. For a grouped bed planting, it uses the group’s space. The next plant must fit its size and shape. If you want different plans for different parts, separate those squares first.

In a container group

Select the container, then choose the planting you want to follow. Its succession belongs to that planting’s space in the container.

Long-term plantings keep their space and do not offer planned successions.

Choose the next plant

  1. Select the current planting in its bed or container group.
  2. Open its Succession section and use the add control.
  3. Review the suggested planting date above the plant choices.
  4. Choose a plant and review its planting method, date, and any warnings.

If the current planting is missing its method or date, complete those details first so timing suggestions have something to work from.

Why the suggested list changes

Suggestions consider the available footprint, timing, plant family, and climate. Changing the date can change which plants are suggested. The suggested list focuses on annual plants and can also exclude choices when reliable timing information is missing.

A short list means fewer plants suit that space and date based on the available guidance. You can remove the suggestion filter to browse more choices, then read the warnings for the one you choose.

Can I choose an earlier date?

Yes, you can review a different date. Keep the current planting in mind: an earlier succession may mean removing it before the end of its expected useful window.

A date suggestion is a planning aid, not an instruction to remove a productive plant. If you plan to keep picking from it, allow more time. If you expect to clear it early, review the date guidance rather than assuming a larger list of choices means there is no tradeoff.

When it is time to plant

Select the succession and use Plant to confirm the actual date.

If the current planting still occupies the space, the confirmation includes its removal and the new planting. You can add separate journal entries for the removal and the planting, with their respective dates.

Removing the current planting through Remove is another route. If Remove planned successions is off, the next succession moves into the space as a planned plant, with later successions still attached. It is not automatically marked as planted. Turn that option on only if you also want to remove all its planned successions.
